# The Homepage — Storyboard & Visual Direction

The homepage is a short painted film carried by the scroll: ten beats, nine of them clips generated from the paintings and played as real video while their beat is on screen, one of them a painting under a slow camera. It is an argument made visually:
**a lawyer assembles → his gown fills with the marks of his trade → order (the gavel) → an orchard of rights → an idea disclosed → a structure built → a certificate stamped → a sapling grown → a constellation resolved into the scales and then into a tree → the tree watered → the website.**

Scroll progress 0–1 runs across a pinned, full-viewport stage (`STAGE_HEIGHT_VH` in `src/components/hero/story.ts`). That file is the single timeline, in two layers. `SCENES` are the eight chapters and carry the copy: ranges, labels, captions and their alignment. `FILM` is the picture: ten beats, each either a `still` (asset id, optional fallback, `drift`) or a `clip` (clip id, fallback still(s), optional `drift`), with the beat's dominant `tone`. `TRANSITIONS` say where and how one beat becomes the next, `ANNOTATIONS` are the labels pinned to the picture, `GAVEL_STRIKE_AT` is where the strike's caption is keyed on the scroll. `frameAt(progress)` resolves all of that into "beat A with its local progress and drift, beat B (during a transition) with its own, blend, kind"; the scroll decides *which* beats are on screen and how far the blend has gone, while a clip runs on its own clock: it starts from its first frame when its beat arrives, plays through, and holds its last frame (or loops) until the visitor scrolls on. A clip that has not been encoded yet, or that the browser cannot play, resolves to its poster or its fallback still, so the story never depends on the pipeline having run. The stills are neoclassical oil-painting images generated with Higgsfield (GPT Image 2, 3:2); the clips are five-second Kling 3.0 videos generated from those stills (image-to-video, 16:9, static camera), upscaled on Higgsfield to 2168 × 1440 at 24 fps and encoded into web video (VP9 and H.264, a large and a phone-sized variant, plus posters). The prompts are in `content/artwork/prompts.json`; the pipeline that turns them into web files is in ARCHITECTURE.md.

The picture is drawn by one of three renderers chosen by `capabilities.ts`:

| Tier | When | Renderer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `webgl` | wherever WebGL works and is hardware-rendered — phones included; this is the default | `hero/painted/PaintedStory.tsx` — a single full-screen fragment shader (`painted/shaders.ts`) with two texture slots, the picture on screen and the one being revealed (each a still or a playing video — a still is uploaded once, a video every time it presents a new frame). It cover-fits around the focal point on landscape screens, contains the whole picture over a blurred ambient surround on portrait screens, drifts the stills like a dolly, and blends with a pigment dissolve, a curtain or a ripple |
| `canvas` | no WebGL, software GL (SwiftShader/llvmpipe), save-data, or the shader failed to compile | `hero/painted/CanvasStory.tsx` — the same film and timeline in 2D: stills drift, clips play as video drawn straight from the element, the dissolve is a cross-fade with a light vertical smear, the ripple a plain cross-fade, the curtain a wavy-edged dark shape with a gold edge; portrait screens contain the picture over a blurred, darkened copy of itself |
| `static` | prefers-reduced-motion (or `?render=static`) | `hero/static/StaticStory.tsx` — no animation, nothing running per frame: one painting per scene (the still of the scene's first beat, or that beat's fallback — never a clip) hung as an editorial sequence with the captions, the map drawn inline as SVG |

Both animated renderers load the film through `painted/media.ts` (one *reel* per beat: whatever is drawable for it right now — the video while it plays, else its poster, else the still), read `progressStore.get().value` each frame (never React state in the render loop), ease toward it, tell the reels which beats are on screen so the clips start, pause and rewind, and call `onReady()` once the first picture is drawable. Force a tier with `?render=webgl|canvas|static`; add `?snap` to disable the easing for screenshots and `?t=<seconds>` to freeze the clips on screen at that second once the page has settled; `?video=0` switches the video off (posters and stills only, transitions intact). Both publish `window.__yilFrame` (progress, beats, mix, and the clip on screen with its clock and state) for QA scripts.

On top of the renderer sit DOM layers, all driven straight from the progress store: `TextOverlay.tsx` (the captions — a `scrim` style gives a side caption the soft backdrop centred captions always have, `wordmark` sets the closing title card large in the middle of the stage), `Annotations.tsx` (registration marks, leader lines and mono labels pinned to points on the picture, one of them the smudged-photocopy humour beat; desktop only), `MapOverlay.tsx` (the vector map of India for the sapling beat; desktop only), `ConstellationOverlay.tsx` (the nine stars, the scales and the tree of the constellation beat; desktop only), the frame marks and the beat counter (`01 / 10`) in `CinematicHome.tsx`, the scroll hint, `SoundToggle.tsx` and, before any of it, `Loader.tsx`. `CinematicHome` also switches the stage between the `surface-lapis` and `paper` surfaces as the beat on screen changes tone (`FILM[].tone`, at the midpoint of a transition), so captions, annotations and controls always sit on the right colours. On phones a scrim in the surface colour rises under the captions.

## Mood

A neoclassical academic oil painting hung in a bright gallery — and, in the clips, that painting come to life with its brushwork intact. Not tenebrism: light is soft daylight from the upper left, the walls are flat and the colour is restrained but luminous. Two worlds share the stage — a deep ultramarine wall (lapis, `#1b5ad6`) for the lawyer's beats (assembly, gown, strike, orchard) and the close (constellation, water), and a flat gallery-white wall for the workshop beats (disclosure, structure, certificate, sapling) — furnished with ivory marble, black silk, crisp white linen, gold-leaf highlights and one crimson accent, the wax seal. Fine painterly brushwork, a faint canvas weave, a restrained varnish sheen, museum quality. The shared style sentence in `prompts.json` is what keeps the stills one set; it goes into every still prompt, as does the "no text" sentence. Every clip prompt opens with "Oil painting come to life, the painterly texture preserved throughout." and closes with "Static camera, no zoom, no text." — the camera never moves inside a clip (the film's camera moves are the stills' drift and the transitions), and no words are ever painted: every word on the stage is DOM.

The site's chrome continues the painting's room: paper by default, lapis for the blue sections, gold and crimson used sparingly, Cormorant Garamond for display, DM Sans for body, IBM Plex Mono for the labels. The pictures are annotated like plates in a technical drawing — hairlines, registration marks in the corners, leader lines, mono captions — which is the tension the homepage lives on: a salon painting read as evidence. Never neon, never a gradient blue, never cartoon, never a stock photograph.

The figure is an **original, generic Indian advocate** — not a likeness of anyone. He is always seen from behind, face turned away: assembling out of dust at the start (`advocate`), standing like a statue while the sapling grows (`statue`), walking toward the light (`legal-world`) and beside the tree at the end (`water-still`), where a second, equally generic figure — a young woman with a notebook — does the watering. The three later stills were generated with the `advocate` still as an image reference so the same man appears throughout; whoever regenerates `advocate` must regenerate the other three the same way, and a clip whose start image changes must be regenerated from the new still. No real place is implied anywhere (the map marker in the sapling beat is conceptual). Tone throughout is educational: the film explains, it does not solicit.

## Beat by beat (progress ranges, media, captions and transitions from story.ts)

Between consecutive beats the picture changes over a window centred on the transition's `at` (width 0.03–0.05); both beats keep playing during the window — A running on (or holding its last frame), B started from its first frame the moment the blend begins — so the film never stalls. A **dissolve** breaks the picture up along a noise field into pigment grains and vertical streaks — thin columns sliding at their own speeds, coarse blocks right at the edge, a few gold motes catching the light, a slight chromatic split — while the next picture settles in behind. A **ripple** spreads a wave from the centre of the frame, displacing the picture along its radius with a thinner ribbon trailing it and light on the crest; the next picture follows the wavefront outward past the corners. The **curtain** (used once, into the gavel) draws a dark cloth with a gold rim light across the frame from the right and pulls it away; as it is pulled away it sends an outward ring through the picture it uncovers. Within a clip beat the video plays on its own clock, from its first frame when the beat arrives, once through to its last frame and then holding it (the loop clips — the gown, the orchard, the structure — run forward, then backwards, indefinitely); a beat the visitor leaves is paused and rewound, so revisiting it replays from the start whichever way the scroll goes. Within a still beat the painting drifts like a camera on a dolly (a zoom of no more than seven percent, pans of a couple of percent, eased from the beat's start to its end); a clip beat gets the same slow push-in (1 → 1.05), which keeps the picture alive after the clip has ended. A clip beat whose video is missing, still loading or refused by the browser shows its poster (the clip's first frame) or its fallback still, under the same drift.

### 1 · Assembly — 0.00 → 0.10 · clip `assembly` (fallback `advocate`) · lapis
The advocate — full length, from behind at a three-quarter angle, one hand holding the gown out to the side like a curtain — assembles out of fine gold dust and pale mist in front of the flat ultramarine wall, from the edges of the gown inward, until he stands whole on the low marble step, the folds catching warm gold light. The clip was generated the other way round (the figure dissolving into dust, which the model does far better than the reverse) and is encoded backwards (`reverse: true` in the manifest); it starts the moment the loader lifts, plays through in five seconds and holds on its last frame — the whole man — for as long as the visitor stays. Caption **left**: IP LITIGATION · DELHI / ADV. ROHIT PRADHAN / "Scroll to begin." (with the two buttons). The scroll hint sits bottom-left until progress 0.02. At 0.10 (window 0.075–0.125) a **ripple** spreads from the centre and the gown follows the wavefront out.

### 2 · The gown fills — 0.10 → 0.34 · clip `gown-fills`, a seamless loop (fallback `gown`) · lapis
Extreme close view of the black silk billowing in slow motion across the blue wall — no figure, no hands, only fabric — warm gold light gliding along the folds; from within the cloth faint gold emblems glow and drift like light catching embroidered thread — small circular seals, a tiny gear, a leaf medallion — and sink back into the silk. The clip is encoded as a forward-then-backward loop, so the silk never stops moving however long the visitor lingers over the five subjects. From the folds the five IP subjects emerge, one every 0.042 of progress from 0.13, each with a caption that **alternates right, left, right, left, right**: Trade Marks, Patents, Copyright, Designs, Geographical Indications, under the eyebrow "Intellectual Property · 01…05". On desktop each subject is also pinned to a point in the fabric by an annotation — a registration mark, a leader line and its glyph (®, ⚙, ©, ◇, ⌖) with the subject's name in mono. **Humour beat:** the first of them, the Trade Marks ® — the one right that is all about exact reproduction — prints like a bad photocopy, blurred, skewed and doubled by a ghost copy, and snaps sharp `SMUDGE_FOR` (0.006) later. At 0.35 (window 0.33–0.37) the **curtain** is drawn across from the right and pulled away, sending a ring through the gavel it reveals; the stage stays lapis.

### 3 · The strike — 0.34 → 0.42 · clip `strike` (fallback `gavel`) · lapis
Low camera angle: a dark walnut gavel with a brass band hangs above its round sound block on the edge of a marble table, a cream document with a small crimson seal beside it, the blue wall above. **Humour beat:** it hangs there a beat too long, trembling slightly as if held, then swings down and strikes; a ring of light and dust bursts across the table and the wall, the seal glints, the gavel settles. The impact follows the clip, not the scroll: the manifest records `impactAt` (3.6 s on the source), the pipeline maps it into the encoded clip's timeline, and the first time the clip's clock crosses it during a play the engine emits one `impact` event — a warm white flash (an envelope that decays over about 600 ms) and, if sound is on, one thud (`sound.gavel()`) happen together. The clip starts as the curtain begins to lift it, so the strike lands a few seconds after the visitor arrives; a visitor who scrolls away and back sees it hang and strike again. The caption is keyed on the scroll at **0.385** (`GAVEL_STRIKE_AT`), **centre** at 0.39: ORDER / "The record is settled." / "Rights are only as real as their enforcement." At 0.42 (window 0.40–0.44) the picture **dissolves** into the orchard.

### 4 · The orchard — 0.42 → 0.49 · clip `orchard`, a seamless loop (fallbacks `orchard`, then `patent`) · lapis

## Performance & motion rules
- One draw call per frame: a single full-screen triangle, one fragment shader, two textures bound (the picture on screen and the one being revealed — a still or a playing video). A still is uploaded once and moved between the slots as the film advances or rewinds; a video is re-uploaded whenever it has presented a new frame (`requestVideoFrameCallback` where the browser has it), with no mipmaps. No post-processing pass and no extra textures — grain, streaks, motes, the chromatic split, the cloth, the ripple and the ambient surround are all procedural in the shader.
- DPR is capped at 2 on every tier — the 2K clips deserve it.
- Progress eases toward the scroll target every frame (`p += (target − p) · dt/90`) on top of ScrollTrigger's `scrub: 0.6`, so fast scrolling still looks cinematic; `?snap` removes the easing for QA screenshots. The renderer runs continuously while any clip is playing or the gavel's flash is decaying, otherwise redraws only when something changed, refreshes only the grain at rest (~20fps) and stops entirely while the stage is off screen (IntersectionObserver).
- Files: stills as `<id>.webp` at 2000px and `<id>-sm.webp` at 1100px; clips as `public/art/film/<id>/clip.webm` (VP9) and `clip.mp4` (H.264) at up to 2560px wide — 2168 × 1440 as the upscaled sources are — with `clip-sm.webm` / `clip-sm.mp4` at up to 1280px, `poster.webp` (1200px) and `poster-sm.webp` (640px) of the first frame. Phones get the small stills (`wantsSmallArt()`: viewport × DPR ≤ 1400) and the small clips (`wantsSmallVideo()`: viewport width × DPR ≤ 1400). Loading (`media.ts`) is progressive: the first beat's picture — its tiny blurred placeholder from `art-manifest.json`, then its poster — before anything else so the loader can lift, its clip preloading meanwhile; then the other posters and stills follow in story order. At most three `<video>` elements exist at a time — the beat on screen, the next (fully preloaded before its transition begins) and the previous (metadata only) — the rest are released; sources are listed webm first, then mp4; every element is muted, `playsinline`, and plays only once the loader has lifted. Until a clip is playing the renderer draws its poster (or the fallback still). `onReady()` fires when the first picture is drawable and its clip can play (or 2.5 s have passed), never later than 4s, and the loader never blocks beyond 3.5s.
- Portrait screens contain the whole picture — the film is never cropped on phones — over the same picture blurred and darkened as ambient light (a still is sampled far down its mip chain; a video frame has no mip chain and is blurred with a wide ring of taps instead, so it reads as a soft surround, never a sharp copy); landscape screens cover-fit around each picture's focal point (a clip uses its still's).
- Scroll is never hijacked. Camera drift is small (scale ≤ 1.07 on stills, a 1 → 1.05 push-in on clips) — the camera inside a clip is static by prompt; the only motion inside a clip is the clip's own. No flashing beyond the single gavel pulse. Video is muted, plays inline and never autoplays with sound; on save-data connections (and with `?video=0`) no video element is created at all and the posters stand in. Every caption holds ≥ 0.025 of progress (roughly 200–250px of scroll on a desktop viewport).
- No text inside the paintings or the clips, ever — every still prompt ends with the "no text" sentence, every clip prompt with "Static camera, no zoom, no text.", and every word on the stage is a DOM element (selectable, translatable, read by screen readers).
- Annotations, the map, the constellation, the frame marks and the beat counter are desktop-only (`md:`); phones keep the captions and get a scrim in the surface colour beneath them.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` → `static` tier. Sound never autoplays: a remembered preference is re-armed only on the visitor's first pointer or key gesture.
- Regenerating a still changes nothing in code: keep the aspect ratio at 3:2, keep the empty wall where the caption sits (left for the assembly and the structure; right for the gown, the orchard, the disclosure and the certificate; the upper half for the strike; the group left and the wall right for the sapling; the figure centre-right for the constellation and the water), set the focal point in `content/artwork/manifest.json`, and let the Fetch artwork workflow do the rest. Regenerating a clip changes nothing in code either — keep it 5 s, 16:9, generated image-to-video from the matching still, upscale it to 2K at 24 fps, and keep `loop: true` on the gown, the orchard and the structure — with one exception, which is still a manifest edit rather than code: a new strike clip lands at a different moment, so `impactAt` in `content/artwork/manifest.json` (seconds on the forward, untrimmed source; today 3.6) must move to the new moment of contact; read it off `public/art/film/strike/sheet.jpg` to get close, then off the video itself in a player.
