# The Homepage — Storyboard & Visual Direction

The homepage is a scroll-driven sequence of eight painted stills. It is an argument made visually:
**a lawyer → his gown → the subjects of intellectual property → order (the gavel) → the machine of patents → design → the mark → the map → the whole legal world → the website.**

Scroll progress 0–1 runs across a pinned, full-viewport stage (`STAGE_HEIGHT_VH` in `src/components/hero/story.ts`). That file is the single timeline: `SCENES` (ranges, labels, captions and their alignment), `ART` (which painting each scene holds, its camera drift and its dominant tone), `TRANSITIONS` (where and how one painting becomes the next), `ANNOTATIONS` (the labels pinned to the paintings) and `GAVEL_STRIKE_AT`. `frameAt(progress)` resolves all of that into "painting A, painting B, blend, kind, drift" and every renderer draws exactly that frame. The paintings themselves are eight neoclassical oil-painting stills generated with Higgsfield (GPT Image 2, 3:2) from the prompts in `content/artwork/prompts.json`; the pipeline that turns them into web files is in ARCHITECTURE.md.

The picture is drawn by one of three renderers chosen by `capabilities.ts`:

| Tier | When | Renderer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `webgl` | wherever WebGL works and is hardware-rendered — phones included; this is the default | `hero/painted/PaintedStory.tsx` — a single full-screen fragment shader (`painted/shaders.ts`) that cover-fits two paintings around their focal points, drifts them slowly, and blends them with a pigment dissolve or a curtain wipe |
| `canvas` | no WebGL, software GL (SwiftShader/llvmpipe), save-data, or the shader failed to compile | `hero/painted/CanvasStory.tsx` — the same paintings and timeline as a 2D cross-fade with a light vertical smear; the curtain is a wavy-edged dark shape with a gold edge |
| `static` | prefers-reduced-motion (or `?render=static`) | `hero/static/StaticStory.tsx` — no animation, nothing running per frame: the paintings hung one after another as an editorial sequence with the eight captions, the map drawn inline as SVG |

Both animated renderers read `progressStore.get().value` each frame (never React state in the render loop), ease toward it, and call `onReady()` once the first painting is drawable. Force a tier with `?render=webgl|canvas|static`; add `?snap` to disable the easing for screenshots.

On top of the renderer sit DOM layers, all driven straight from the progress store: `TextOverlay.tsx` (the captions), `Annotations.tsx` (registration marks, leader lines and mono labels pinned to points on the paintings; desktop only), `MapOverlay.tsx` (the vector map of India for scene 7; desktop only), the frame marks and scene counter (`01 / 08`) in `CinematicHome.tsx`, the scroll hint, `SoundToggle.tsx` and, before any of it, `Loader.tsx`. `CinematicHome` also switches the stage between the `surface-lapis` and `paper` surfaces as the painting on screen changes tone (at the midpoint of a transition), so captions, annotations and controls always sit on the right colours. On phones a scrim in the surface colour rises under the captions.

## Mood

A neoclassical academic oil painting hung in a bright gallery. Not tenebrism: light is soft daylight from the upper left, the walls are flat and the colour is restrained but luminous. Two worlds share the stage — a deep ultramarine wall (lapis, `#1b5ad6`) for the lawyer's scenes (1, 2, 3 and 8) and a flat gallery-white wall for the machine scenes (4–7) — furnished with ivory marble, black silk, crisp white linen, gold-leaf highlights and one crimson accent, the wax seal. Fine painterly brushwork, a faint canvas weave, a restrained varnish sheen, museum quality. The shared style sentence in `prompts.json` is what keeps the eight stills one set; it goes into every prompt, as does the "no text" sentence — no words are ever painted, every word on the stage is DOM.

The site's chrome continues the painting's room: paper by default, lapis for the blue sections, gold and crimson used sparingly, Cormorant Garamond for display, DM Sans for body, IBM Plex Mono for the labels. The paintings are annotated like plates in a technical drawing — hairlines, registration marks in the corners, leader lines, mono captions — which is the tension the homepage lives on: a salon painting read as evidence. Never neon, never a gradient blue, never cartoon, never a stock photograph.

The figure is an **original, generic Indian advocate** — not a likeness of anyone. He is always seen from behind, face turned away: in scene 1 holding the gown open like a theatre curtain, in scene 8 walking toward the light. Scene 8 was generated with the scene 1 painting as an image reference so the same man appears in both; whoever regenerates one must regenerate the other the same way. No real place is implied anywhere (the map marker in scene 7 is conceptual).

## Scene by scene (progress ranges, captions and transitions from story.ts)

Between consecutive scenes the picture changes over a window centred on the transition's `at` (width 0.045–0.05). A **dissolve** breaks the painting up along a noise field into pigment grains and vertical streaks — thin columns sliding at their own speeds, coarse blocks right at the edge, a few gold motes catching the light, a slight chromatic split — while the next painting settles in behind. The **curtain** (used once, after the gavel) draws a dark cloth with a gold rim light across the frame and pulls it away. Within a scene the painting drifts like a camera on a dolly: a zoom of no more than ten percent and a pan of a few percent, eased from the scene's start to its end (`ART[].drift`).

The left half of the wall is empty. The painting settles from a slight zoom to rest (1.06 → 1.0). Caption **left**: IP LITIGATION · DELHI / ADV. ROHIT PRADHAN / "Scroll to begin." The scroll hint sits bottom-left until progress 0.02. At 0.10 the picture **dissolves** into the gown.

## Performance & motion rules
- One draw call per frame: a single full-screen triangle, one fragment shader, two textures bound (the painting on screen and the one being revealed). No post-processing pass and no extra textures — grain, streaks, motes, the chromatic split and the cloth are all procedural in the shader.
- DPR is capped at 1.5 on the `webgl` tier (the dissolve samples noise per pixel) and at 2 on `canvas`.
- Progress eases toward the scroll target every frame (`p += (target − p) · dt/90`) on top of ScrollTrigger's `scrub: 0.6`, so fast scrolling still looks cinematic; `?snap` removes the easing for QA screenshots. At rest the loop only refreshes the grain (~20fps) and it stops entirely while the stage is off screen (IntersectionObserver).
- Images: `<id>.webp` at 2000px for desktops and `<id>-sm.webp` at 1100px for phones (`wantsSmallArt()`: viewport × DPR ≤ 1400). They load progressively — the tiny blurred placeholder from `art-manifest.json` first, then the first painting, then the rest in story order. `onReady()` fires when the first painting is drawable and never later than 4s, and the loader never blocks beyond 3.5s.
- Scroll is never hijacked. Camera drift is small (scale ≤ 1.1, pans of a few percent). No flashing beyond the single gavel pulse. Every caption holds ≥ 0.025 of progress (roughly 200–250px of scroll on a desktop viewport).
- No text inside the paintings, ever — every prompt ends with the "no text" sentence and every word on the stage is a DOM element (selectable, translatable, read by screen readers).
- Annotations, the map, the frame marks and the scene counter are desktop-only (`md:`); phones keep the captions and get a scrim in the surface colour beneath them.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` → `static` tier. Sound never autoplays: a remembered preference is re-armed only on the visitor's first pointer or key gesture.
- Regenerating a painting changes nothing in code: keep the aspect ratio at 3:2, keep the empty wall where the caption sits (left for scenes 1 and 5, right for 2, 4, 6 and 7, the upper half for 3, centre-right figure for 8), set the focal point in `content/artwork/manifest.json`, and let the Fetch artwork workflow do the rest.
