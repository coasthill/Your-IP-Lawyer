# The Homepage — Storyboard & Visual Direction

The homepage is a short painted film scrubbed by the scroll: ten beats, seven of them clips generated from the paintings and played frame by frame, three of them paintings under a slow camera. It is an argument made visually:
**a lawyer assembles → his gown fills with the marks of his trade → order (the gavel) → an orchard of rights → an idea disclosed → a structure built → a certificate stamped → a sapling grown → a constellation resolved into the scales and then into a tree → the tree watered → the website.**

Scroll progress 0–1 runs across a pinned, full-viewport stage (`STAGE_HEIGHT_VH` in `src/components/hero/story.ts`). That file is the single timeline, in two layers. `SCENES` are the eight chapters and carry the copy: ranges, labels, captions and their alignment. `FILM` is the picture: ten beats, each either a `still` (asset id, optional fallback, `drift`) or a `clip` (clip id, fallback still(s), optional `keyframes` mapping progress-within-beat to a frame fraction, optional `hold`), with the beat's dominant `tone`. `TRANSITIONS` say where and how one beat becomes the next, `ANNOTATIONS` are the labels pinned to the picture, `GAVEL_STRIKE_AT` is the moment of impact. `frameAt(progress, frameCount)` resolves all of that into "beat A at frame *n* with its drift, beat B at frame *m*, blend, kind" and every renderer draws exactly that frame; a clip whose frames are not in the repository yet resolves to its fallback still, so the story never depends on the pipeline having run. The stills are neoclassical oil-painting images generated with Higgsfield (GPT Image 2, 3:2); the clips are five-second Kling 3.0 videos generated from those stills (image-to-video, 16:9, static camera) and sliced into WebP frames at 10 fps. The prompts are in `content/artwork/prompts.json`; the pipeline that turns them into web files is in ARCHITECTURE.md.

The picture is drawn by one of three renderers chosen by `capabilities.ts`:

| Tier | When | Renderer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `webgl` | wherever WebGL works and is hardware-rendered — phones included; this is the default | `hero/painted/PaintedStory.tsx` — a single full-screen fragment shader (`painted/shaders.ts`) with two texture slots, the picture on screen and the one being revealed (each a still or a clip frame), re-uploaded only when the drawable behind a slot changes. It cover-fits around the focal point on landscape screens, contains the whole picture over a blurred ambient surround on portrait screens, drifts the stills like a dolly, and blends with a pigment dissolve, a curtain or a ripple |
| `canvas` | no WebGL, software GL (SwiftShader/llvmpipe), save-data, or the shader failed to compile | `hero/painted/CanvasStory.tsx` — the same film and timeline in 2D: stills drift, clips play frame by frame, the dissolve is a cross-fade with a light vertical smear, the ripple a plain cross-fade, the curtain a wavy-edged dark shape with a gold edge; portrait screens contain the picture over a blurred, darkened copy of itself |
| `static` | prefers-reduced-motion (or `?render=static`) | `hero/static/StaticStory.tsx` — no animation, nothing running per frame: one painting per scene (the still of the scene's first beat, or that beat's fallback — never a clip frame) hung as an editorial sequence with the captions, the map drawn inline as SVG |

Both animated renderers load the film through `painted/media.ts` (one *reel* per beat: whatever is drawable for it right now), read `progressStore.get().value` each frame (never React state in the render loop), ease toward it, and call `onReady()` once the first picture is drawable. Force a tier with `?render=webgl|canvas|static`; add `?snap` to disable the easing for screenshots. Both publish `window.__yilFrame` (progress, beats, mix, the frame actually drawn) for QA scripts.

On top of the renderer sit DOM layers, all driven straight from the progress store: `TextOverlay.tsx` (the captions — a `scrim` style gives a side caption the soft backdrop centred captions always have, `wordmark` sets the closing title card large in the middle of the stage), `Annotations.tsx` (registration marks, leader lines and mono labels pinned to points on the picture, one of them the smudged-photocopy humour beat; desktop only), `MapOverlay.tsx` (the vector map of India for the sapling beat; desktop only), `ConstellationOverlay.tsx` (the nine stars, the scales and the tree of the constellation beat; desktop only), the frame marks and the beat counter (`01 / 10`) in `CinematicHome.tsx`, the scroll hint, `SoundToggle.tsx` and, before any of it, `Loader.tsx`. `CinematicHome` also switches the stage between the `surface-lapis` and `paper` surfaces as the beat on screen changes tone (`FILM[].tone`, at the midpoint of a transition), so captions, annotations and controls always sit on the right colours. On phones a scrim in the surface colour rises under the captions.

## Mood

A neoclassical academic oil painting hung in a bright gallery — and, in the clips, that painting come to life with its brushwork intact. Not tenebrism: light is soft daylight from the upper left, the walls are flat and the colour is restrained but luminous. Two worlds share the stage — a deep ultramarine wall (lapis, `#1b5ad6`) for the lawyer's beats (assembly, gown, strike, orchard) and the close (constellation, water), and a flat gallery-white wall for the workshop beats (disclosure, structure, certificate, sapling) — furnished with ivory marble, black silk, crisp white linen, gold-leaf highlights and one crimson accent, the wax seal. Fine painterly brushwork, a faint canvas weave, a restrained varnish sheen, museum quality. The shared style sentence in `prompts.json` is what keeps the stills one set; it goes into every still prompt, as does the "no text" sentence. Every clip prompt opens with "Oil painting come to life, the painterly texture preserved throughout." and closes with "Static camera, no zoom, no text." — the camera never moves inside a clip (the film's camera moves are the stills' drift and the transitions), and no words are ever painted: every word on the stage is DOM.

The site's chrome continues the painting's room: paper by default, lapis for the blue sections, gold and crimson used sparingly, Cormorant Garamond for display, DM Sans for body, IBM Plex Mono for the labels. The pictures are annotated like plates in a technical drawing — hairlines, registration marks in the corners, leader lines, mono captions — which is the tension the homepage lives on: a salon painting read as evidence. Never neon, never a gradient blue, never cartoon, never a stock photograph.

The figure is an **original, generic Indian advocate** — not a likeness of anyone. He is always seen from behind, face turned away: assembling out of dust at the start (`advocate`), standing like a statue while the sapling grows (`statue`), walking toward the light (`legal-world`) and beside the tree at the end (`water-still`), where a second, equally generic figure — a young woman with a notebook — does the watering. The three later stills were generated with the `advocate` still as an image reference so the same man appears throughout; whoever regenerates `advocate` must regenerate the other three the same way, and a clip whose start image changes must be regenerated from the new still. No real place is implied anywhere (the map marker in the sapling beat is conceptual). Tone throughout is educational: the film explains, it does not solicit.

## Beat by beat (progress ranges, media, captions and transitions from story.ts)

Between consecutive beats the picture changes over a window centred on the transition's `at` (width 0.03–0.05); both beats keep playing during the window — A on its last frames, B on its first — so the film never stalls. A **dissolve** breaks the picture up along a noise field into pigment grains and vertical streaks — thin columns sliding at their own speeds, coarse blocks right at the edge, a few gold motes catching the light, a slight chromatic split — while the next picture settles in behind. A **ripple** spreads a wave from the centre of the frame, displacing the picture along its radius with a thinner ribbon trailing it and light on the crest; the next picture follows the wavefront outward past the corners. The **curtain** (used once, into the gavel) draws a dark cloth with a gold rim light across the frame from the right and pulls it away; as it is pulled away it sends an outward ring through the picture it uncovers. Within a clip beat, progress maps linearly to the clip's frames unless `keyframes` or `hold` say otherwise; within a still beat the painting drifts like a camera on a dolly (a zoom of no more than seven percent, pans of a couple of percent, eased from the beat's start to its end). A clip beat whose frames are missing plays its fallback still, motionless — a clip carries no drift of its own.

### 1 · Assembly — 0.00 → 0.10 · clip `assembly` (fallback `advocate`) · lapis
The advocate — full length, from behind at a three-quarter angle, one hand holding the gown out to the side like a curtain — assembles out of fine gold dust and pale mist in front of the flat ultramarine wall, from the edges of the gown inward, until he stands whole on the low marble step, the folds catching warm gold light. The clip was generated the other way round (the figure dissolving into dust, which the model does far better than the reverse) and is played backwards (`reverse: true` in the manifest); `hold: [0.85, 1]` plays it over the first 85 % of the beat and freezes on the last frame — the whole man — for the rest. Caption **left**: IP LITIGATION · DELHI / ADV. ROHIT PRADHAN / "Scroll to begin." (with the two buttons). The scroll hint sits bottom-left until progress 0.02. At 0.10 (window 0.075–0.125) a **ripple** spreads from the centre and the gown follows the wavefront out.

### 2 · The gown fills — 0.10 → 0.34 · clip `gown-fills` (fallback `gown`) · lapis

### 3 · The strike — 0.34 → 0.42 · clip `strike` (fallback `gavel`) · lapis

## Performance & motion rules
- One draw call per frame: a single full-screen triangle, one fragment shader, two textures bound (the picture on screen and the one being revealed — a still or a clip frame). A texture is re-uploaded only when the drawable behind its slot changes; when the revealed picture becomes the one on screen the slots swap instead. No post-processing pass and no extra textures — grain, streaks, motes, the chromatic split, the cloth, the ripple and the ambient surround are all procedural in the shader.
- DPR is capped at 1.5 on the `webgl` tier (the dissolve samples noise per pixel) and at 2 on `canvas`.
- Progress eases toward the scroll target every frame (`p += (target − p) · dt/90`) on top of ScrollTrigger's `scrub: 0.6`, so fast scrolling still looks cinematic; `?snap` removes the easing for QA screenshots. The renderer redraws whenever the frame changes (a new beat or a new frame index), refreshes only the grain at rest (~20fps) and stops entirely while the stage is off screen (IntersectionObserver).
- Files: stills as `<id>.webp` at 2000px and `<id>-sm.webp` at 1100px; clip frames as `public/art/film/<id>/fNNN.webp` at up to 1200px (1176 × 780 as Kling delivers them) and `sm/fNNN.webp` at 640px; phones get the small set (`wantsSmallArt()`: viewport × DPR ≤ 1400). Loading (`media.ts`) is progressive: the first still — its tiny blurred placeholder from `art-manifest.json`, then the file — before anything else so the loader can lift; then the frames of the opening beat begin and the other stills follow in story order. Frames load sequentially, at most four in flight, the beat on screen first, then the next, then the previous; the renderer draws the nearest loaded frame and, until a clip's first frame arrives, the beat's fallback still. `onReady()` fires when the first picture is drawable and never later than 4s, and the loader never blocks beyond 3.5s.
- Portrait screens contain the whole picture — the film is never cropped on phones — over the same picture blurred (sampled far down the mip chain) and darkened as ambient light; landscape screens cover-fit around each still's focal point (clip frames are centred).
- Scroll is never hijacked. Camera drift is small (scale ≤ 1.07, pans of a couple of percent) and clips carry no drift of their own — the camera inside a clip is static by prompt. No flashing beyond the single gavel pulse. Every caption holds ≥ 0.025 of progress (roughly 200–250px of scroll on a desktop viewport).
- No text inside the paintings or the clips, ever — every still prompt ends with the "no text" sentence, every clip prompt with "Static camera, no zoom, no text.", and every word on the stage is a DOM element (selectable, translatable, read by screen readers).
- Annotations, the map, the constellation, the frame marks and the beat counter are desktop-only (`md:`); phones keep the captions and get a scrim in the surface colour beneath them.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` → `static` tier. Sound never autoplays: a remembered preference is re-armed only on the visitor's first pointer or key gesture.
- Regenerating a still changes nothing in code: keep the aspect ratio at 3:2, keep the empty wall where the caption sits (left for the assembly and the structure; right for the gown, the orchard, the disclosure and the certificate; the upper half for the strike; the group left and the wall right for the sapling; the figure centre-right for the constellation and the water), set the focal point in `content/artwork/manifest.json`, and let the Fetch artwork workflow do the rest. Regenerating a clip changes nothing in code either — keep it 5 s, 16:9, 10 fps, generated image-to-video from the matching still — with one exception: a new strike clip lands at a different moment, so the `0.68` in the strike beat's `keyframes` must move to the new impact frame's fraction (its 0-based index ÷ (frames − 1)); read it off `public/art/film/strike/sheet.jpg` or the frames themselves.
